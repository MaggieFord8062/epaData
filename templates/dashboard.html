Read the charts. A few things to know.

Where every number comes from

Select any point, bar, box, state or cell on a chart to open its calculation: the value, the totals it was made from (for example total CO2 ÷ total gross load), how many records, units, facilities and years went into it, anything left out and why, and a link that opens exactly those records in Explore. About, at the top right of every chart, explains what it shows, how its values are calculated, why that kind of chart was chosen and how to read it.

Totals and ratios

Amounts that add up (emissions, gross load, heat input) are shown as totals. Ratios such as CO2 intensity and heat rate are shown as overall ratios, the total of one divided by the total of the other, so large units count more than small ones.

Colors

Each color always means the same thing on every chart: CO2, coal and group A are blue; SO2, gas and group B are orange; NOx and oil and other fuels are green; gross load is yellow. Maps use one blue from light (low) to dark (high). The colors were checked to stay distinguishable for color-blind readers, and every chart also has its numbers in a table.

Log scales

A few very large plants emit far more than most units, so distributions and scatter plots use a log scale by default, where each gridline is ten times the one before. Values of 0 can't be shown on a log scale and are counted in the chart notes.

Correlation

Correlation shows whether two measurements tend to move together. It does not show that one causes the other.
